Property Buying Documents Checklist in Haryana

Loan purchase • self-funded purchase • builder documents • BBA • registry • title chain verification
Use this page before paying a major amount. The purpose is to help a buyer understand which documents to ask for, what each document is meant to establish, and what should be independently verified.

1. First understand the three document buckets

Buyer documents

Your identity, address, income and banking documents. These become especially important when a bank loan is involved.

Property documents

Documents establishing title, approvals, project status, agreement terms, payments and possession/registration.

Verification documents

Records used to cross-check ownership, land particulars, encumbrances, approvals and the continuity of the title chain.

2. If you are buying the property with a home loan

Exact requirements vary by lender, applicant profile and property type. Ask the bank for its current checklist.

Buyer / borrower documentsTypical purpose
PAN, Aadhaar or other accepted KYCIdentity and KYC verification.
Address proofCurrent/permanent address verification.
Salary slips / income proofIncome and repayment-capacity assessment for salaried applicants.
Form 16 / ITR and financial statementsIncome verification, especially where applicable for salaried/self-employed applicants.
Bank statementsFinancial and repayment assessment.
Employment/business proofApplicant and income-profile verification where required.

Property documents the lender may ask for

  • Allotment Letter / booking documents
  • Agreement for Sale / Builder-Buyer Agreement (BBA), where applicable
  • Title documents and previous title chain
  • Approved layout / sanctioned building plan, where applicable
  • RERA registration and project disclosures, where applicable
  • Sale / Conveyance Deed or draft document for registration
  • Encumbrance / charge-related information
  • Possession Letter and OC/CC, where applicable
  • Payment receipts / statement of account

Important: A bank's legal/technical approval is not a substitute for the buyer's own due diligence. Understand what the bank has actually verified and what remains your responsibility.

3. If you are buying without a loan

When there is no bank financing, do not assume that the absence of a loan means fewer property checks are needed. The buyer should independently verify title, approvals, agreement terms, payments and registration/possession status.

4. What should you ask the builder for before signing BBA / Agreement for Sale?

  • RERA registration details, where applicable
  • Land title document and relevant title chain
  • Latest applicable land/revenue records
  • Encumbrance / charge details
  • Licence / CLU / development permissions, where applicable
  • Approved layout plan
  • Sanctioned building plan, where applicable
  • Project approvals and NOCs applicable to the project
  • Collaboration / JDA / development agreement if the developer is not the land owner
  • Draft Allotment Letter
  • Draft Agreement for Sale / BBA
  • Payment plan and complete cost sheet
  • Unit specifications, area statement and promised amenities
  • Possession timeline and applicable conditions
  • Any project-specific disclosures or authority correspondence that materially affects the purchase
Do not rely only on a sales brochure. Important promises about price, area, specifications, possession, parking, amenities or other obligations should be traceable to the contractual/official documents that apply to your purchase.

5. Registry-day checklist

Before the final registration, compare the final deed against the documents you signed and the property you are actually buying.

  • Final Sale Deed / Conveyance Deed draft
  • Buyer and seller/developer identity details
  • Allotment Letter and Agreement/BBA
  • Relevant previous title documents
  • Property/unit description, area, floor, unit number and boundaries
  • Consideration/payment details and payment receipts
  • Stamp duty and registration-fee calculation
  • Possession Letter, where applicable
  • OC/CC, where applicable
  • No-dues / payment clearance information, where applicable
  • Authority/company authorization documents, where required
  • Any required NOC, permission or transfer document applicable to the property
After registry: keep the registered deed, payment proof, possession records and all relevant approvals/documents together in a permanent property file. Also understand what mutation or other post-registration record updates are required for your property type.

6. The property title chain — what does “chain” mean?

A title chain is the sequence of documents showing how the relevant ownership/right in the property moved from an earlier owner to the current seller/developer and ultimately to the buyer.

1. Original / earlier owner

Identify the person/entity from whom the relevant title originates.

2. Transfer document

Check the registered sale, conveyance, gift, partition or other applicable instrument.

3. Subsequent transfers

Repeat the check for every material transfer until the current owner/developer.

4. Developer rights

If a developer is not the land owner, verify the development/collaboration/JDA arrangement and authority to develop/sell.

5. Your purchase

Your allotment/agreement and final conveyance should connect clearly to the property and the party legally entitled to transfer it.

7. How to verify the title chain

CheckWhat to match
NamesOwner/seller/developer names across deeds, approvals and agreements.
Property identityKhasra/survey details, plot/unit number, area, boundaries and project/phase details as applicable.
Dates & registrationDocument dates, registration details and logical sequence of transfers.
ContinuityThere should be no unexplained gap between the relevant transfer documents.
AuthorityConfirm that the person/entity signing your transaction has the legal/contractual authority to do so.
EncumbrancesCheck for recorded mortgage/charge or other interests and obtain clarification/release documents where applicable.
ApprovalsMatch the actual property/project with the approvals and sanctioned plans that apply to it.

Best practice: For a high-value transaction, ask an independent property lawyer to conduct a title search and review the complete chain rather than relying only on documents supplied by the seller or builder.

8. Quick “before I pay” checklist

  • ☐ I know who legally owns/controls the property right being sold.
  • ☐ I have reviewed the relevant title chain.
  • ☐ I have checked RERA status where applicable.
  • ☐ I have seen the applicable approvals and sanctioned plans.
  • ☐ I have read the Agreement/BBA, not just the brochure.
  • ☐ I understand the total price and every major additional charge.
  • ☐ I have checked possession/OC/CC status where applicable.
  • ☐ I have verified payment receipts and outstanding dues.
  • ☐ I have independently addressed any mortgage, charge, dispute or title concern.
  • ☐ For a high-value transaction, I have obtained independent legal review.
